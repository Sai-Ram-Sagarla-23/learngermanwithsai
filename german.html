Introduction to German

History, where German is spoken, CEFR levels, and your study roadmap.

Where German Is Spoken

German is a West Germanic language with ~100 million native speakers — the EU’s most spoken native language. Official in 6 countries.

6 German-Speaking Countries
Deutschland
Germany — 83M speakers
Österreich
Austria — 9M
die Schweiz
Switzerland — 5M (of 4 langs)
Luxemburg, Liechtenstein, Belgien
Also official
German vs. English

✅ Similarities: Shared vocab (Haus, Wasser, Brot), cognates (Musik, Telefon), same alphabet +4 special chars.

⚡ Key differences:

  • 3 genders: der/die/das for every noun
  • 4 cases change article endings
  • Verb always in position 2 (main clauses)
  • Verb goes to END in subordinate clauses
  • ALL nouns are capitalised

Grammar Foundations

Articles, grammatical gender, noun rules, and plural patterns.

The Three Genders
der
Masculine
der Mann, der Tisch
die
Feminine
die Frau, die Stadt
das
Neuter
das Kind, das Buch
⚠️ Plural: ALL genders use die die Männer, die Frauen, die Kinder — all use die in plural.
Masculine (der) patterns
  • Male persons: der Mann, der Vater
  • Days, months, seasons: der Montag, der März
  • -er agent nouns: der Lehrer, der Fahrer
  • -ismus: der Tourismus
Feminine (die) patterns
  • Female persons: die Frau, die Mutter
  • -ung: die Zeitung, die Wohnung
  • -heit/-keit: die Freiheit, die Möglichkeit
  • -tion/-ie: die Nation, die Familie
Neuter (das) patterns
  • -chen/-lein diminutives: das Mädchen, das Häuschen
  • Infinitives as nouns: das Essen, das Lernen
  • -um/-ment: das Museum, das Experiment
  • Young animals: das Baby, das Lamm

Sentence Structure

The V2 rule, questions, negation, imperatives, and subordinate clauses.

The V2 Rule — Verb Always in Position 2
Position 1Position 2 (VERB)Rest
Ichlernejeden Tag Deutsch.
Jeden Taglerneich Deutsch. (subject moved!)
Morgenfahreich nach Berlin.
💡 When you front anything besides the subject, subject and verb invert. "Heute lerne ich" — NOT "Heute ich lerne".
Questions
Sprichst du Deutsch?
Do you speak German? (yes/no)
Wo wohnst du?
Where do you live? (W-question)
Wie viel kostet das?
How much does that cost?
Negation: nicht vs. kein
Ich arbeite nicht.
I do not work. (negates verb)
Ich habe kein Geld.
I have no money. (negates noun)
Er ist kein Arzt.
He is not a doctor.
Imperatives (Commands)
Targetlernenkommenessen
du (sing.)Lern(e)!Komm!Iss!
ihr (pl.)Lernt!Kommt!Esst!
Sie (formal)Lernen Sie!Kommen Sie!Essen Sie!
Subordinate Clauses — Verb Goes to END!
Key rule After weil, dass, wenn, ob, obwohl... the verb goes to the very end.
Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Berlin wohnen möchte.
I learn German because I would like to live in Berlin.
Er sagt, dass er müde ist.
He says that he is tired.

German Tenses

All 6 tenses with structure, usage, and examples.

1. Präsens — Present

Covers: current actions, habits, general truths, near future (with time adverb).

Ich lerne jeden Tag.
I learn every day.
Morgen fliege ich nach Wien.
Tomorrow I'm flying to Vienna. (near future)
2. Perfekt — Most Common Spoken Past

Structure: haben / sein + Partizip II

sein or haben? Use sein with motion verbs: gehen, kommen, fahren, werden, bleiben, sterben. All others → haben.

InfinitivePartizip IIAuxiliary + Example

sehengesehenhaben — Wir haben gesehen.
seingewesensein — Ich bin müde gewesen.
3. Präteritum — Written/Narrative Past

Used in novels, newspapers, formal writing. In speech: mainly sein, haben, modals.

4. Plusquamperfekt — Past Perfect
Als ich ankam, hatte er schon gegessen.
When I arrived, he had already eaten.
5. Futur I — werden + Infinitive

Goethe Exam Prep

Strategies for all four skills: Reading, Writing, Listening, Speaking.

📖 Reading (Lesen)
  • Read headlines first, then scan for key info
  • 100% comprehension not needed — focus on main ideas
  • Practice: DW (Deutsche Welle), Spiegel, taz.de
  • Signal words: jedoch, allerdings, außerdem, deshalb
✍️ Writing (Schreiben)
  • Plan before writing (5 min outline)
  • Use connectors: Erstens... Außerdem... Schließlich...
  • For formal: use Sie, no contractions, formal vocabulary
  • Always check V2 word order!
🎧 Listening (Hören)
  • Read questions before audio plays
  • Note keywords, not full sentences
  • Practice: Deutschlandfunk, DW “Langsam gesprochene Nachrichten”
  • Focus on tone and context when words are unclear
🗣️ Speaking (Sprechen)
  • Paraphrase if you forget a word: “Das Ding, mit dem man...”
  • Use fillers: Also..., Ich glaube..., Meiner Meinung nach...
  • Don’t stop for mistakes — keep going
  • Practice: shadowing, record yourself, italki, Tandem

German Culture

Germany, Austria, Switzerland — festivals, etiquette, work culture.

German Culture Essentials
  • Pünktlichkeit: Punctuality is crucial. Being late is considered rude.
  • Direct communication: Germans are honest and direct. "No" means no.
  • Sunday laws: Most shops closed on Sundays (Ladenschlussgesetz).
  • Mülltrennung: Strict recycling — Papier, Plastik, Biomüll, Restmüll, Glas.
  • Greetings: Handshake standard when meeting strangers.
  • du vs. Sie at work: Startups use du; traditional firms use Sie.
Austria & Switzerland
  • Austria: Strong coffee house culture, classical music, Alps. “Grüß Gott” instead of “Hallo”.
  • Switzerland: 4 official languages — German, French, Italian, Romansh.
  • Swiss German: Very different dialect — hard to understand even for Germans!
  • Direct democracy: Regular citizen referendums on important issues.

Fluency Training

Shadowing, thinking in German, daily routines, and fluency milestones.

The Shadowing Technique

Shadowing means listening to native German audio and repeating it simultaneously, mimicking rhythm, speed, and intonation exactly. It is the most effective spoken fluency technique.

How to Shadow Effectively

▼
  1. Choose audio at your level
  2. Listen once without repeating
  3. Listen again reading the transcript
  4. Shadow: speak at the same time as the recording
  5. Record yourself and compare
  6. 15–30 minutes daily for rapid progress

Best Resources by Level

▼
  • A1–A2: Nicos Weg (ARD alpha), Slow German podcast by Annik Rubens
  • B1–B2: Deutsche Welle (DW), Easy German YouTube
  • C1–C2: Deutschlandfunk radio, Der Spiegel podcasts, Bundestag speeches
